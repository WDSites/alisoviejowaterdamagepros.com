Aliso Viejo, our home base

Aliso Viejo, ZIP 92656, is where our office is located at 32 Journey. The city is a master-planned community of about 6,600 acres that incorporated in 2001, and Moulton Niguel Water District supplies its water. See the home page for services and local details.

Cities we serve

Laguna Niguel, ZIP 92677, is a neighboring city with a long master-planned history. Our Laguna Niguel page covers its parks, flood notes and permit contact. Laguna Hills, ZIP 92653, borders Aliso Viejo, and the Laguna Hills page explains its creeks and its two water districts.

Mission Viejo, ZIPs 92691 and 92692, is the largest city in this group, and the Mission Viejo page covers its lake, dams and three water districts. Laguna Beach, ZIP 92651, has canyon and slope exposure, which is covered on the Laguna Beach page. Lake Forest, ZIPs 92630 and 92610, has creek flood zones noted on its Lake Forest page.

Other nearby places

We also serve Dana Point, ZIP 92629, San Juan Capistrano, ZIP 92675, Rancho Santa Margarita, ZIP 92688, and Irvine. These places do not have their own pages, and the dispatcher can help over the phone. Call the 24/7 line and give your address.

How the six cities differ for water damage

Each place has a different mix of water risks. Laguna Niguel and Laguna Hills are mostly about interior leaks and storm runoff on slopes. Mission Viejo adds a private lake and three state-inspected dams in its safety element. Laguna Beach has canyon drainage that its own draft safety plan calls a recurring flooding problem, and Lake Forest has creek flood zones named in a city environmental report.

Aliso Viejo, where our office is located, sits in the Aliso Creek watershed, and the city Public Works department is listed as its floodplain contact. These differences change what we ask when you call and what we look for when a technician arrives.

Find your water district

Water districts are not the same as city boundaries. Mission Viejo is split three ways, Laguna Hills is split between two districts and Lake Forest is mainly served by Irvine Ranch Water District. The simplest way to find yours is to look at your water bill, and that district is who to ask when a main shut-off valve fails or when you smell sewer gas.

Check your flood map and your permits

The Federal Emergency Management Agency publishes a flood map lookup for any address, and it is the right first check if storm water has entered your home. For rebuild permits, each city has its own building office. Laguna Beach and Mission Viejo state that permits are needed before repairing a building, while Laguna Niguel, Laguna Hills and Lake Forest direct you to their building divisions for specifics.

Not sure we serve your address? Call and ask.

What the dispatcher asks about your address

When you call, the dispatcher asks for the street, the nearest cross street and whether the building is a house, condo or rental. Those details tell us which city office applies, which water district to name if a shut-off fails and whether an HOA controls the shared walls. If the road is steep, gated or closed, say so, because the crew plans the route from what you tell us.

Not sure whether your address is covered? Call and the dispatcher will confirm.

What stays the same in every city

The number, the dispatch process and the order of work do not change from one city to the next. A dispatcher asks where the water is coming from, whether it is still running, what the water touched and which rooms are wet. Crews then stop or confirm the source, remove standing water, measure moisture and dry the structure before any repair is priced.

How we choose what to publish

We list only towns we can describe accurately, with ZIP codes confirmed from more than one source and utilities and permit offices taken from city and district pages. If a source does not say something, the page says to check your bill, check the FEMA flood map or ask the city building department.

What happens when you call

The dispatcher asks where the water came from, what is wet and where you are, and tells you what to shut off. A crew is arranged and a technician explains scope and price before work starts. For more on services, see the services page.
