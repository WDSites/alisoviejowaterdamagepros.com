Water in a business space is a different job

A leak in a home usually involves one owner and one policy. A leak in a business space often involves a tenant, a landlord, a building manager and two insurers, all with different views of who pays and who gives the go-ahead. The water does not wait for that conversation, so the first call should cover both the wet materials and the people who must approve the work.

Aliso Viejo has office buildings, and the City of Aliso Viejo Major Projects page shows that several of its active projects replace existing office buildings. That is a modest fact, but it matches what we see on the phone. Many calls come from a single suite inside a larger building, where the source of the water may be on another floor or in a shared wall.

First-hour safety steps

Safety comes before saving the carpet. Water above a ceiling grid can carry weight until a tile drops, and water near lighting, copiers or floor outlets can energize a surface. Keep staff and visitors away from sagging tiles and wet floors, and do not stand in water to reach a panel.

  • Close the nearest shut-off valve if you know where it is, or ask the building manager to close the supply to the floor.
  • Turn off power to the wet area only from a dry panel. If the panel is wet, leave it and call your utility or 911.
  • Call the building manager or landlord, since shared lines and ceilings are often theirs to control.
  • Photograph the ceiling, walls and floor before anything is moved, then call our 24/7 dispatch line.
  • Lift computers, files and inventory off the floor, but leave heavy furniture where it is.

Landlord, tenant and the lease

Who handles the repair depends on the lease, not on us. Read the maintenance and insurance sections, then call the building manager and ask who authorizes emergency work in the suite and in shared areas. Ask for the answer in writing by email. Tenants often control the inside of the suite while the landlord controls the structure, the roof and the lines in the walls.

We do not decide that question and we do not describe your coverage. We dry what is wet, record what we find and give both sides the same documents. Clear moisture readings and dated photos make the conversation between tenant, landlord and insurer shorter.

Shared walls, ceilings and drying with the business open

Water crosses suite lines through shared walls, ceiling plenums and floor slabs. A leak in one suite can leave damp drywall in three. We map moisture past the visible stain so the neighboring space is checked and the building manager has the full picture. We also ask for access to the adjoining space, which only the landlord or the other tenant can approve.

Many businesses cannot close for several days. Air movers are loud and dehumidifiers draw power, so we plan around outlets and circuits, place equipment along walls and keep walkways clear. A partly open space is possible when the wet area can be separated from the working area, though some jobs need the room cleared while equipment runs.

Ceiling tiles, carpet and tile floors

Suspended ceiling tiles soak up water and often stain, sag or drop. They are usually replaced, and the grid above is checked for wet insulation. Commercial carpet may be dried in place after extraction, or lifted and the pad replaced. Hard tile and sealed concrete dry faster at the surface, but water can hide under tile edges and at the base of walls, so we check there as well.

Records for the insurer or landlord

We record dates, readings, photos and the list of materials removed. If you are asking about lost business days or a rent question, ask your insurer what applies. We do not describe coverage. The California Department of Insurance advises reporting damage to your agent or representative and keeping a log of calls, and that habit helps in a business claim as well.

What changes the cost

The size of the wet area, the type of water, the materials involved, the number of drying days, after-hours access, the amount of demolition and any need to work around an open business all affect the price. A single wet suite with clean water is a smaller job than water that crossed three suites. We explain scope and price before work starts. Same-day response is offered when available.

Permits, repairs and what we leave to others

Roof, plumbing and sprinkler repairs are done by other trades, and we coordinate with them once the source is stopped. If rebuilding involves permits, the City of Aliso Viejo Building and Safety desk can be reached at (949) 425-2540. New businesses moving into a commercial tenant space must also complete a Business Registration with the Community Development Department, which matters if a long closure leads to a move. The city says it has no business license program.

The Orange County Fire Authority lists Aliso Viejo as a member city and runs Station 57 at 57 Journey. It also describes fire safety inspections and code enforcement. We have no information on how alarm or sprinkler systems respond to water, so ask the building manager and the fire authority about those systems.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Waiting for the lease conversation to finish before drying starts.
  • Leaving wet ceiling tiles in place because they look dry on top.
  • Cleaning only your side of a shared wall.
  • Throwing out materials before photographing them.

Common questions

Do you handle commercial water damage at night or on weekends in Aliso Viejo?

Yes. The dispatch line is open 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays. Call with the suite, the source and what is wet. We can also arrange after-hours access with your building manager. Same-day response is offered when available.

Who is responsible for a leak in my suite, the landlord or the tenant?

It depends on the lease and on where the water came from. Read the maintenance and insurance sections, call the building manager and ask for the answer in writing. We dry the space and keep records for both sides.

Can you dry the space while my business stays open?

Sometimes. If the wet area can be separated, we place equipment along walls and plan around circuits. Air movers are loud, so some jobs need the room cleared while the equipment runs.

Do you repair the roof, plumbing or sprinkler lines?

No. Those repairs are done by other trades. We remove the water, dry the space and coordinate with the plumber, roofer or fire protection contractor so the source is fixed.

What records do you provide for my insurer or landlord?

We keep dated photos, moisture readings and a list of materials removed. Share them with your insurer or landlord. For questions about business interruption, ask your insurer what applies to your policy.
