What we do

Aliso Viejo Water Damage Pros is a water damage restoration and mold remediation service for Aliso Viejo, California, and nearby cities in Orange County. We take the first call, send a crew and explain what needs to happen before work begins. Our services are water mitigation, water damage cleanup, water damage repair, flood damage restoration, emergency water removal and mold remediation.

We work from 32 Journey in Aliso Viejo. The address is in the same area as City Hall and the Aliso Viejo Library, and dispatch serves neighborhoods across the city.

How we work

Every job starts with a call. The dispatcher asks what is wet, where the water came from and whether any power or gas is involved. You then hear what to shut off while a crew is arranged. After that, a technician checks moisture, explains scope and price and begins work only after you understand both.

Standards we point to

The industry publishes two standards that homeowners can ask about. ANSI/IICRC S500 covers professional water damage restoration, and ANSI/IICRC S520 covers mold remediation. We name them so you can ask any company, including us, how crews work and what records you will get.

We do not claim awards, review counts, years in business or certifications that we cannot show you. Work is carried out by licensed and insured restoration professionals, and you can verify any California contractor license with the Contractors State License Board.

What we do not do

We do not repair plumbing, waterproof foundations or restore fire damage. If water is entering because of a grading, foundation or roofing defect, a specialist in that trade should fix the cause so the damage does not come back. We handle the restoration after the water is stopped.

Local base, local knowledge

Aliso Viejo is a planned community of about 6,600 acres that incorporated in 2001, and it sits inside the 36 square mile Aliso Creek watershed. Moulton Niguel Water District supplies its water, which is imported through the Metropolitan Water District. We use those public facts to explain what a leak or a storm can do here, and we name the source on each page so you can check it.

The city is mostly newer construction compared with older Orange County towns, but age still varies by neighborhood. Water heaters, braided supply hoses and washing machine valves fail on their own schedules, and the dispatcher will ask about the age and location of whatever failed.

Owners, renters and HOAs

Census QuickFacts counts 19,421 households in Aliso Viejo and shows 56.6 percent owner-occupied. That means a large share of our calls involve a tenant, a landlord or a homeowners association. We suggest telling the property manager in writing the same day, taking dated photos and asking who authorizes drying in shared walls and floors.

Records you should receive

  • Dated photos of the affected rooms before and after equipment is placed.
  • Moisture meter readings taken at the start of the job and at each visit.
  • A written scope that explains what will be removed, dried and rebuilt.
  • A list of materials removed and contents that were moved or discarded.
  • Drying logs that show when the structure reached normal readings.

Why our pages name their sources

Water damage advice goes wrong when it is vague. When a page says a Census figure, a National Weather Service record or a utility report, we name the type of source so you can look it up yourself. When we could not find a fact, we say so and send you to your water bill, the FEMA flood map or your city building office. We would rather give you a short honest answer than a long guess.

The same rule covers prices. We do not publish numbers we cannot support, so we explain the factors that move cost and give scope and price before work begins. That is also why you will not find testimonials, star ratings or award badges here. If a claim cannot be shown, it is not on the page.

Privacy and records
