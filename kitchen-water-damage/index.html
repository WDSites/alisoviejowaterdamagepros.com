Why kitchens lose so much to a small leak

A kitchen holds more water lines in one room than almost any other. The sink has two supply lines and a drain trap, the dishwasher has a supply and a drain, the refrigerator may feed an ice maker and the disposal sits under it all. Most of those connections hide behind a cabinet door, so a slow leak can run for weeks.

The Environmental Protection Agency WaterSense program says about nine percent of homes have leaks that waste 50 gallons or more per day, and that a faucet dripping once a second can waste more than 3,000 gallons a year. Water that size does not just vanish. It goes into the cabinet base and the floor.

Where Aliso Viejo kitchens leak

Census QuickFacts lists 19,421 households in Aliso Viejo, about 56.6 percent owner-occupied, with a median owner-occupied home value of $863,800 and a median gross rent of $2,774. Those numbers matter in the kitchen, because cabinets, counters and flooring are costly to replace, and a good dry-out can save material that would otherwise be torn out.

The Moulton Niguel Water District 2023 water quality report lists hardness at 13.3 grains per gallon. Hard water leaves mineral crust at faucets and supply lines, and a white crust at a connection is a clue that it has been seeping. We do not assume how old a home or its plumbing is.

Safety and first steps at the sink

Close the shut-off valve under the sink or behind the appliance. If you cannot find it, close the main water valve for the home. Unplug the dishwasher or refrigerator if the cord is dry and out of the water, and keep power off any outlet that water has reached.

  • Empty the cabinet under the sink and set out towels to catch drips.
  • Do not run the dishwasher or disposal until a plumber checks the line.
  • Photograph the leak, the cabinet and the floor before moving anything.
  • Tell the neighbor below if water may be dripping into their unit.
  • Call us for drying and a plumber for the pipe repair.

Cabinets, toe-kicks and what can be saved

Cabinet boxes are built of particleboard, plywood or solid wood, and the material decides how they react to water. Particleboard swells and crumbles once it is soaked and often cannot be dried back to shape. Plywood and solid wood handle a short soaking much better. We check what your cabinets are made of before making a call.

The choice is often dry in place or pull the toe-kick. Removing the toe-kick board lets air move behind the cabinets and shows whether water got under them. It is a small opening that can save a large amount of cabinetry. If the base is badly swollen, the crew explains which sections need to come out before a rebuild.

How we dry a kitchen

We start with moisture meters on the cabinets, walls and floor, then use a thermal camera to trace the wet area. Extraction units pull standing water from the floor. Air movers and dehumidifiers run behind and under cabinets, and wet insulation behind them is removed. We use ANSI/IICRC S500 as a reference for water damage drying.

Flooring depends on the type. Tile and grout dry well in most cases, but water can sit under the tile and in the subfloor. Wood or laminate may cup, and vinyl can trap moisture underneath. Island and countertop seams are checked as well, since water can move up into the core.

Mold risk and the 24 to 48 hour window

The Environmental Protection Agency advises drying water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to help prevent mold growth. Kitchens are warm, and a closed cabinet traps humidity. A musty smell under the sink days after a leak is a reason to call. Our appliance leak water damage page covers the machine itself.

Condos, shared stacks and who calls the plumber

In a condo or apartment, a kitchen drain often shares a stack with the units above and below. A clog or leak in that stack can come from a neighbor, and the water may also reach their ceiling. Tell your property manager or HOA in writing the same day and ask who authorizes the plumbing work. We dry your unit, and we keep photos and readings for your claim.

What changes the cost

Cost depends on the size of the wet area, how many cabinets and floor sections are affected, the flooring type, how many drying days follow, whether insulation or drywall comes out and the water category. We explain scope and price before work starts.

Insurance, permits and contractor rules

A California Department of Insurance claims guide says homeowners policies do not cover flood but do cover other kinds of water damage. It says to stop leaks at the source right away and warns that delay in cleanup can result in mold. Policies often treat a sudden burst differently from a slow seep, so ask your insurer.

We did not find a published city rule on whether kitchen water damage repair needs a permit. Ask the City of Aliso Viejo Building and Safety office at (949) 425-2540, or check the online permit portal. The Contractors State License Board says unlicensed work must stay under $1,000 as of January 1, 2025, and only when no workers are hired and no permit is needed.
