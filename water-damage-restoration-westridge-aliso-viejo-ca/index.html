First steps after a roof leak or heavy rain

  • Stop the source

    The source here is often rain and not a pipe, so there may be nothing to shut off. If a ceiling is bulging with water, a small hole can let it drain into a bucket, and the roof itself is for a roofer.

  • Power safety

    Water above a ceiling can reach light fixtures and wiring. Stay out of rooms with a sagging, dripping ceiling and call 911 or the utility if you see sparks or smell burning.

  • Photograph and protect

    Photograph the stain, the ceiling and any wet floor, and move furniture and electronics away from the drip. Place a bucket under active drips, and do not climb onto a wet roof.

  • Keep people out

    Keep people and pets out from under a sagging ceiling, which can drop wet drywall and insulation without warning.

Sandbags and storm preparation

The Orange County Fire Authority lists sand and sandbags at Station 57, 57 Journey in Aliso Viejo, and at Station 22 in Laguna Hills, and asks residents to bring a shovel. A city notice from October 2026 points residents to Station 22. Sandbags help direct surface water, but they do not fix a roof leak or an indoor plumbing failure.

Gated sections and HOAs

Listing sites describe some Westridge sections as gated. If yours is, tell the gate or the management office that a crew may arrive, and give the dispatcher the entry instructions. If water crossed into a shared wall or a neighbor's unit, notify the HOA the same day.

Insurance and documentation

A rain loss may be treated differently from a plumbing loss. The California Department of Insurance notes that flood insurance covers heavy rainfall and overflowing water, which standard homeowners policies do not usually cover, so ask your insurer how yours treats rain entering the home. Keep dated photos of the stain as it changes.
