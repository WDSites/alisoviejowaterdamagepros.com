How a burst pipe floods a home here

A burst pipe is a split, a failed joint or a pinhole that lets pressurized water escape. Pressure is the reason it is so destructive. A line that runs at full house pressure keeps pushing water into drywall, insulation and flooring until somebody closes a valve.

Aliso Viejo has a lot of two-story homes and condo buildings, so water seldom stays where it starts. It follows framing, drops through light fixtures and shows up as a stain or a drip on the level below. Winter freezing is uncommon in this part of Orange County, so most failures here come from wear, corrosion, a bad fitting or a pipe knocked loose during other work.

The Moulton Niguel Water District reports an average hardness of 13.3 grains per gallon in its 2023 water quality report, and all of its drinking water is imported through the Metropolitan Water District. Hard water leaves mineral crust, and a crusty green or white buildup at a joint is a clue worth showing your plumber. We cannot say it causes a failure, only that it often marks a slow leak.

Supply line or drain, wall, ceiling or slab

A supply line carries clean water under pressure, so a break is usually Category 1 at the start. A drain or waste line carries used water, and a leak there is treated as Category 2 or 3 under the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard. The first question we ask is which kind of pipe it is, because it changes how much material comes out.

A wall break soaks the bottom plate and baseboard first. A ceiling break often needs a hole cut to let trapped water drain and to open the cavity for air movers. A slab leak is different, because water can sit under flooring and in the subfloor layers where it is hard to see.

The Moulton Niguel Water District troubleshooting guide says a meter dial still moving with the toilet supply valves off points to an indoor leak, possibly a slab leak.

Emergency safety steps in the first hour

Stop the water before anything else. The Moulton Niguel Water District troubleshooting guide says the house valve is typically at the front of the home, and an older or corroded valve may need to be turned slowly. For most homes the meter sits in the front yard near the sidewalk.

If the meter dial keeps moving with every fixture off, you likely have a leak. If it moves with the house valve closed, the leak may be on the line before the house.

If a problem looks like it is on the street side of the meter, the district lists a 24/7 water or sewer emergency line at (949) 831-2500. Call it for a main-side problem, and call us for the water already inside your walls. Keep away from outlets, the breaker panel and light fixtures that are dripping.

  • Close the valve

    Turn the house valve clockwise, slowly if it is old. Then open a low faucet to relieve pressure in the line.

  • Power safety

    Switch off breakers for wet rooms only if the panel is dry and you do not stand in water. Otherwise call your utility or 911.

  • Protect what you can

    Lift rugs, electronics, books and small furniture. Put foil or blocks under heavy legs. Leave heavy pieces where they are.

  • Record it

    Photograph the wet walls, the pipe if you can see it and the water line on furniture before anything is moved or thrown out.

What we do and what your plumber does

The division of labor is simple. A plumber finds the break, cuts out the failed section and replaces it. Our crew removes the water, finds where it went and dries the building so nothing has to be torn out that did not need to be.

We start with extraction units to lift standing water from carpet, pad and hard floors. A moisture meter and a thermal camera then map wet drywall, framing and flooring, including areas that feel dry to the touch. Wet insulation and low sections of drywall are removed when they cannot dry in place. Air movers and dehumidifiers then run until readings come down to a normal level, and we recheck before equipment leaves. You will see the readings.

Condo leaks, upstairs neighbors and HOA notice

If the pipe is in the unit above you, the water reaches your ceiling before you know the source. Tell your HOA or property manager the same day and ask them in writing who authorizes work on shared lines. Take photos of every wet surface.

Census QuickFacts lists 19,421 households in the city and an owner-occupied rate of 56.6 percent, so many leaks involve a renter or a neighbor.

What changes the cost

Cost depends on how long the water ran, how many rooms and floors it reached and the materials it soaked. Hardwood, laminate and cabinets dry slowly. The water category matters, and so does the amount of demolition needed to reach wet cavities. More days of drying equipment add to the total. We explain scope and price before work starts.

Insurance and the cause of the burst

A California Department of Insurance guide lists sudden and accidental water damage among the losses typically covered, and it lists seepage and wear and tear among those usually not covered. A pipe that splits suddenly is a different story from one that has dripped for months. Report the loss to your agent soon and ask what your policy says. The same guide says to keep a log of calls.

Mistakes to avoid after a pipe bursts

Do not wait to see if a stain dries on its own. Do not run a household vacuum on wet carpet or pad. Do not close up a wall that has been wet. Do not ignore a slow, hidden leak because the bill looks only a little high, since the Moulton Niguel Water District guide invites customers who find the cause of high use to file a bill adjustment request.

The Environmental Protection Agency has a research record on copper pitting that notes pinhole leaks are underreported and affect many water utilities across the country. That record is not specific to Southern California, so we do not claim it describes local homes. It is a reason to treat any repeated pinhole as a sign to have the whole line checked.

Common questions

Is emergency burst pipe water damage service available in Aliso Viejo at night or on weekends?

Yes. The 24/7 line takes calls at night, on weekends and on holidays. Describe where the water is and what is wet, and the dispatcher tells you what to shut off while a crew is arranged. Same-day response is offered when available.

Do you fix the broken pipe?

No. A plumber replaces the failed pipe. We remove the water, dry the walls, ceiling and floor and clean up afterward. We can point out where the water appears to start, and that note helps the plumber find the break faster.

How can I tell if I have a leak inside the walls?

Turn off every fixture and watch the water meter dial. If it keeps moving, water is going somewhere. The district guide says movement with the toilet supply valves off points to an indoor leak, possibly under the slab.

Who do I call if the leak is before my meter?

Moulton Niguel Water District lists a 24/7 line for water or sewer emergencies at (949) 831-2500. Call it for a main-side problem. Call us for water that has already entered the building.

Will my homeowners policy cover a burst pipe?

Sudden and accidental water damage is typically covered, while slow seepage and wear are usually not. Policies vary, so ask your agent. We keep dated photos and moisture readings that you can share with your insurer.
