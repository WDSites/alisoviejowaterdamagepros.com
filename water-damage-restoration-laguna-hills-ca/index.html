Where Laguna Hills sits and why it matters for water

According to the city General Plan, Laguna Hills is bordered by Irvine and Lake Forest to the north, Interstate 5 and Mission Viejo to the east, Laguna Niguel to the south and Aliso Viejo and Laguna Woods to the west. The city has 30,020 residents (Census 2025 estimate) in about 6.55 square miles, and 70.0 percent of homes are owner-occupied. That density puts a lot of water heaters, washers and supply lines in a small area.

Creeks, reservoirs and the flood map

The city Safety Element maps 100-year floodplains around Aliso Creek, Veeh Reservoir and Mill Creek, plus an area northwest of Alicia Parkway in the south. It lists lower-risk areas along the La Paz Channel and North Sulphur Creek near Moulton Parkway. It also says Lake Mission Viejo, Upper Oso Reservoir and El Toro Reservoir could send flood flows down Oso Creek toward the southeast edge of the city near Interstate 5.

That language is a planning document, not a promise about any one street. If you want to know where your address stands, check the FEMA flood map or ask the city. For storm water that has already entered the home, read about flood damage restoration before anyone walks through it.

When water is spreading in Laguna Hills

When water is spreading in Laguna Hills, shut off the supply first and then call the 24/7 line. We will tell you what to do while a crew is arranged, and same-day response is offered when available. Never wade through water near a panel or outlet.

  • Stop the source

    Close the main water valve or the valve at the fixture. If the supply line is in a condo wall, tell the HOA so shared water can be shut off.

  • Power safety

    Turn off power to wet rooms only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without standing in water. Call 911 or your utility for any electrical or gas risk.

  • Lift and photograph

    Lift valuables onto furniture and take photos of the water line before the crew arrives.

  • Keep people out

    Keep children and pets out of any water that came from a toilet, drain or sewer, then call the 24/7 line for Laguna Hills.

Rebuilding in Laguna Hills

The city FAQ says the Building Department is at City Hall, 24035 El Toro Road, and lists permitted construction hours of Monday through Friday 7 a.m. to 8 p.m. and Saturday 8 a.m. to 8 p.m., with no work on Sundays or federal holidays. Those hours matter for repair crews, so confirm the current rules with the department.

Repair work follows drying. If drywall, flooring or trim needs to be replaced, water damage repair covers the rebuild, and water damage cleanup handles contaminated debris first.

Two water districts, one city

El Toro Water District serves portions of Laguna Hills and Moulton Niguel Water District also lists Laguna Hills. The boundary inside the city is not given on the pages we checked, so look at your water bill to see which district serves your home. Ask that district about shut-off help if a main valve fails.

Landmarks and neighborhoods

Laguna Hills Community Center, at 25555 Alicia Parkway with its Town Green, and Laguna Hills Mall are well-known places in the city. Sheep Hills Park in the Westside annexation is owned by the Aliso Viejo Community Association, which shows how closely the two cities are tied. If you live near the mall or the civic center, tell us your cross streets when you call.

Common questions

Is emergency water removal available in Laguna Hills?

Yes. Dispatch runs around the clock, and the 24/7 line takes calls at night, on weekends and on holidays. Same-day response is offered when available, and the dispatcher tells you what to shut off first.

Do you cover every ZIP code in Laguna Hills?

We serve Laguna Hills ZIP 92653, from the Alicia Parkway corridor near the Community Center to the El Toro Road side by City Hall. Note that 92653 is also used by parts of Aliso Viejo and Laguna Woods, which are separate cities.

Is Laguna Hills the same as Laguna Woods?

No. They are separate cities. El Toro Water District serves the entire city of Laguna Woods, while Laguna Hills is split between El Toro and Moulton Niguel water districts.

Does Laguna Hills have flood zones?

The city Safety Element maps 100-year floodplains around Aliso Creek, Veeh Reservoir, Mill Creek and a portion northwest of Alicia Parkway. Check the FEMA flood map for your address.

Who should I call at the city about repair permits?

The Building Department at City Hall. Ask which repairs after water damage need a permit.
