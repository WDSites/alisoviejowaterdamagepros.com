What storm water damage is, and how it differs from a flood

This page is about the storm as the cause. Wind-driven rain pushed past a slider, a gutter that overflowed down a wall, a patio drain that backed up and runoff that slipped under a garage door all count. Rising water that fills a home belongs on our flood damage restoration page.

The two overlap, and the water is often Category 2 once it has crossed soil or a driveway, so crews treat it with care. Our 24/7 line is open on nights and weekends because most storms arrive after dark. Same-day response is offered when available.

  • Stop the entry

    Close doors and windows, and push towels against a leaking slider or garage door. Do not stand in water near outlets.

  • Clear what you can

    Move rugs, boxes and electronics off the floor. Lift cardboard first because it wicks water fast.

  • Mind the power

    Leave the breaker alone if the panel is wet or you must stand in water to reach it. Call your utility or 911 for any electrical risk.

  • Document

    Photograph the water line, the entry point and the weather damage outside before cleanup starts.

Storm hazards in Aliso Viejo

The City of Aliso Viejo Local Hazard Mitigation Plan lists flooding, landslide and severe storms among its hazards of concern. It also marks two 2023 federal declarations, EM-3591-CA and EM-3592-CA, as having affected the city. The plan points to heavy rainfall between October and April and says windstorms are common during Santa Ana wind conditions.

The City General Plan Safety Element says Aliso Viejo is at risk of flooding from surface drainage through streets and storm drains, and that most 100-year flood zones lie at the base of canyons and hills. The National Weather Service describes the January 9 and 10, 2023 atmospheric river as bringing 3 to 6 inches of rain to Orange County foothills.

Getting ready for this season

In October 2026 the City of Aliso Viejo posted a notice saying a very strong El Nino is expected in fall and winter 2026 to 2027, with flood risk highest in low-lying areas. The notice asks residents to clean leaves and debris from gutters before rain and to sign up for AlertOC, the county alert system.

It also says sand and sandbags are available at OCFA Fire Station 22 in Laguna Hills, at 24001 Paseo de Valencia, and that you should bring your own shovel because staff may not be there. The OCFA sandbag page lists no Aliso Viejo station and tells Aliso Viejo residents to call their city. The Orange County Public Works sandbag program covers unincorporated residents, so it likely does not apply here.

  • Clear gutters, downspouts and patio drains before the first storm.
  • Check that grading and drains carry water away from the house and garage door.
  • Look at slider tracks, window seals and weep holes for gaps.
  • Set up sandbags near garage doors and low entries before rain, not during it.
  • Sign up for AlertOC and keep our number where you can find it at night.

Safety steps in the first hour

Safety comes before cleanup. Do not walk through water near outlets, do not use household vacuums on standing water and keep children and pets out of the wet area. If a storm has downed a power line or the panel is wet, stay clear and call the utility or 911.

If you smell sewage or the water came from a drain, treat it as contaminated. Wear boots and gloves if you must enter, and leave the rest to a crew. After you are safe, call us so the extraction and drying can begin.

How we clean up after a storm

The crew extracts standing water with pumps and extraction units, then checks floors, baseboards and low wall sections with moisture meters and a thermal camera. Wet carpet pad and soaked cardboard are removed. Air movers and dehumidifiers run until readings return to normal. We use ANSI/IICRC S500 as the reference for categories of water and drying goals.

Where the water carried soil, we clean and treat hard surfaces. Items that cannot be cleaned are listed for your records. A roofer, gutter contractor or yard contractor fixes the cause outside, and we handle the damage inside.

Check the map, not a guess

We do not say whether a particular address is in a flood zone. Search the FEMA flood map for your address and ask the City of Aliso Viejo Public Works department at (949) 425-2533, which is listed as the city floodplain contact. A standard homeowners policy usually does not cover flood, so ask your insurer what applies to storm water.

Costs and insurance after a storm

Cost depends on the water category, the area and depth, the materials soaked, the number of drying days and how much demolition is needed. We explain scope and price before work starts. The California Department of Insurance says to report damage right away, keep a log of calls and stop the leak at the source when you can.

After the storm passes

Walk the house and the yard once it is safe. Look for damp patches on ceilings, soft baseboards, musty smells in closets and water marks at the base of walls. A wet spot found in the first days is far cheaper to dry than mold found weeks later. The Environmental Protection Agency advises drying water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours.

Common questions

Is emergency storm water damage cleanup available in Aliso Viejo at night or on weekends?

Yes. The 24/7 line is open on nights, weekends and holidays, including during storms. Describe where the water came in and which rooms are wet. Same-day response is offered when available.

Where can I get sandbags near Aliso Viejo?

The City of Aliso Viejo notice points residents to OCFA Fire Station 22 in Laguna Hills, at 24001 Paseo de Valencia. Bring your own shovel. Supply is limited, so check the City or OCFA before you go.

Is storm water damage the same as flood damage?

They overlap. Storm damage here means water that came in because of weather, such as wind-driven rain or a blocked drain. Rising water that fills a home is covered on the flood damage page.

Will homeowners insurance cover storm water damage?

It depends on how the water entered. The California Department of Insurance says homeowners policies do not cover flood but do cover other kinds of water damage. Report the loss promptly and ask your insurer.

How do I know if my home is in a flood zone?

Search the FEMA flood map for your address and call the City of Aliso Viejo Public Works department at (949) 425-2533. We do not make flood zone calls for individual addresses.
