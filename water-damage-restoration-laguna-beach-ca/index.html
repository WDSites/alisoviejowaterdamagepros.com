Canyons, slopes and storm water in Laguna Beach

Laguna Beach sits between the coast and steep canyons, and its own planning documents say so plainly. The draft Safety Element says flooding is a recurring event, most storm drains are designed for a 25-year flood and the Laguna Canyon Flood Control Channel at various points can handle only between 5 and 43 percent of the water from a 100-year flood. The draft also lists high-risk slopes on either side of Laguna, Bluebird and Aliso canyons.

For a homeowner, that translates into drainage that matters more than elsewhere in south Orange County. Patio drains, garage doors and lower-level rooms are the first places storm water enters. If water has reached your home, call the 24/7 line and read about flood damage restoration.

Slope history that explains the stakes

The USGS caption for the Bluebird Canyon landslide, dated June 1, 2005, says 350 homes were evacuated and 15 were damaged or destroyed, after heavy rains in January and February 2005. The city hazard mitigation plan says the October 1993 Laguna Fire burned over 16,000 acres and destroyed 441 buildings in and near Laguna Beach, and that landslides followed after only mild rain.

We do not repair slopes or foundations. If a slide or drainage failure is sending water into your house, a geotechnical or drainage professional is the right next call, and we handle the water, the drying and the repair afterward.

When water is spreading in Laguna Beach

Call the 24/7 line when water is still moving, when it has entered a lower level or garage or when mud or sewage is involved. Same-day response is offered when available. If the road is closed or the slope is moving, call 911 first.

  • Stop the source

    Close the main water valve or the valve at the fixture. If the supply line is in a condo wall, tell the HOA so shared water can be shut off.

  • Power safety

    Turn off power to wet rooms only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without standing in water. Call 911 or your utility for any electrical or gas risk.

  • Lift and photograph

    Lift valuables off the floor, move vehicles and stored boxes if it is safe, and photograph the water line before the crew arrives.

  • Keep people out

    Keep children and pets out of any water that came from a toilet, drain or sewer, then call the 24/7 line for Laguna Beach.

Coastal climate and rain timing

The National Weather Service weather guide says coastal fog can leave beaches about 15 degrees cooler than a mile inland and that most rain falls from November through March. Plan roof, window and drain checks before November, because a small leak found early is a drying job, not a rebuild. Humidity near the coast also makes closets and bath walls slower to dry.

Two water districts in one city

The city says Laguna Beach County Water District supplies most of Laguna Beach and South Laguna is served by South Coast Water District. Your water bill shows which one serves your home, and that district can help with shut-off questions. The sewer provider was not named on the pages we checked.

Permits and where to ask

The city Community Development Department says residents and businesses must obtain permits before constructing, enlarging, altering, repairing, moving, demolishing or changing a building or an electrical, mechanical or plumbing system. The page does not say whether water damage repairs specifically need one, so ask Community Development. Its permit counter hours were listed as Monday through Thursday, 7:30 a.m. to 2 p.m.

Places we know in Laguna Beach

Main Beach, Laguna Canyon Road and the Crystal Cove State Park portion served by the water district are among the places the city and utility pages name. A February 2019 storm closed Laguna Canyon Road and flooded Highway 133 according to a news report, which is a reminder that access can change fast. Give the dispatcher your cross streets and note any road closures.

Common questions

Is emergency water removal available in Laguna Beach?

Yes. Dispatch runs around the clock, and the 24/7 line takes calls on nights, weekends and holidays. Tell the dispatcher about road access and where the water entered, and a crew is arranged. Same-day response is offered when available.

Do you cover every ZIP code in Laguna Beach?

We serve Laguna Beach ZIP 92651, from the Main Beach and downtown area up the canyons toward Laguna Canyon Road. Some ZIP databases list Laguna Beach as an alias for a Laguna Niguel ZIP, and that area is covered on the Laguna Niguel page.

Does Laguna Beach have flood risk?

The city draft Safety Element says flooding is a recurring event, and the Laguna Canyon Flood Control Channel can handle only 5 to 43 percent of a 100-year flood at various points. Check the FEMA flood map for your address.

Can you fix a slope or drainage problem?

No. We restore the water damage. A geotechnical or drainage professional should evaluate slope or drainage problems.

Do I need a permit for repairs?

The city says permits are required before repairing a building or an electrical, mechanical or plumbing system. Ask the Community Development Department about your specific repair.
