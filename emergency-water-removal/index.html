When standing water is an emergency

Standing water is an emergency whenever it is more than a puddle, whenever it is still coming from a pipe or appliance and whenever it is near electrical outlets or the breaker panel. Water spreads across a floor fast, and a few hours of soaking is the difference between a dry-out and a rebuild.

Our 24/7 line is open at night, on weekends and on holidays. Same-day response is offered when available. Never wade through water that might reach a powered outlet or appliance, and call your utility or 911 for any gas or electrical risk before you call us.

  • Stop the source

    Close the main water valve, or the supply valve for the fixture or appliance that is leaking.

  • Power safety

    Turn off power to the wet area only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without standing in water.

  • Lift what you can

    Move electronics, paper, shoes and small furniture to dry ground. Leave heavy furniture in place.

  • Document

    Take photos or video of the water line on walls and furniture before extraction begins.

How water extraction works

Extraction removes bulk water before drying begins. Submersible pumps handle deep water, and portable or truck-mounted extraction units pull water from carpet, pad and hard floors. The goal is to take out as much liquid as possible so the drying equipment only has moisture to deal with, not gallons.

After extraction, the crew checks moisture with a meter, lifts wet pad if needed and sets air movers and dehumidifiers. If the water stayed on the floor for a long time, the structure may need water mitigation for several days afterward.

Burst pipe water damage

A burst pipe is the most common reason for an emergency call. Copper pinholes, a split supply line behind a toilet, a frozen or failed connector under a sink and a broken irrigation or water heater line all release water quickly. In multi-story buildings, the water often shows up in the unit or room below before anyone knows where it started.

Step one is the shut-off. Close the main valve, open a low faucet to drain the line and keep the wet area clear. Step two is extraction, followed by moisture mapping to find water that moved into wall cavities and under cabinets. A plumber repairs the pipe, and the restoration crew handles the water.

Why speed matters and what it costs

The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth. The cost of a removal job depends on the depth and area of the water, the water category, the materials soaked, how many drying days follow and any demolition. We explain scope and price before work starts, and the dispatcher can give you an idea from a short description.

Insurance and photos

Sudden and accidental water from a pipe or appliance is often covered by a homeowners policy, but policies differ. The California Department of Insurance says to report damage to your insurance agent or representative to start a claim and to keep a log of calls. We keep dated photos and moisture readings so you have records to share.

Common questions

Do you handle water removal emergencies at night or on weekends in Aliso Viejo?

Yes. The 24/7 line is open on nights, weekends and holidays. Call, describe the water and what is already wet, and the dispatcher tells you what to shut off while a crew is arranged. Same-day response is offered when available.

Should I turn off the power if there is water on the floor?

Only if you can reach the breaker panel without stepping in water. If the panel is wet or the area near it is flooded, stay out and call your utility or 911.

Can I use a wet-dry vacuum myself?

For a very small spill, yes. For anything bigger, a household vacuum leaves moisture in the pad and subfloor, which starts the mold clock. Call a crew when the water covers more than a small area.

Does water removal include drying?

Extraction removes the bulk water and drying equipment follows. The two are different steps, and a floor that feels dry after extraction can still be wet underneath.

What if the water came from a neighbor upstairs?

Call us to extract your side and tell your property manager or HOA the same day. Take photos of every wet surface and ask them in writing who authorizes the work.
