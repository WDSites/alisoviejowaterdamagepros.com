Where Glenwood sits in Aliso Viejo

The Aliso Viejo zoning code lists Glenwood as Specific Plan district SP-3, next to Vantis, The Commons and Ventana Ridge. That makes Glenwood one of the few neighborhood names the city itself uses. We did not find an official boundary map, so this page describes Glenwood by what the sources say and not by street lines.

Developer material for the Veranda homes calls Glenwood a golf course community built around a course designed by Jack Nicklaus. It calls Veranda the last golf course community in Aliso Viejo. Confirm with your HOA whether your address falls inside the Glenwood association.

Homes in Glenwood and what that means for a leak

The builder press release describes the Veranda houses as three to five bedroom single-family homes of about 3,167 to 3,758 square feet, with a grand opening in June 2012. Listing sites also show attached condominium and townhome sections in the area. A larger house has more bathrooms, more fixtures and often a second floor, so one supply leak can reach several rooms before anyone sees it.

Citywide, the 2022 Housing Element says 35 percent of Aliso Viejo housing is single-family detached, 26 percent is single-family attached and 39 percent is multifamily. It also says more than two-thirds of the housing stock was built after 1990. A newer home can still leak, because supply lines, water heaters and appliances fail at any age.

It ties flooding to heavy rain between October and April combined with hillside topography, and it lists the 2023 storm declaration EM-3591-CA among events that affected the city. The plan does not single out Glenwood.

What to do first when water is spreading

  • Stop the source

    Close the valve at the fixture, or the main valve for the house, if you can reach it without stepping through water. Know where the main valve is before you ever need it.

  • Power safety

    If the breaker panel is in a wet garage or hallway, do not wade to it. Stay out and call 911 or the utility for sparks, a burning smell or a gas odor.

  • Photograph and protect

    Photograph wet walls, floors and furniture before anything is moved. Lift small valuables, rugs and electronics onto dry surfaces.

  • Keep people out

    Keep children and pets out of water from a toilet or drain. Household fans and vacuums are not enough for that kind of water.

How drying works in a larger home

A technician finds the wet areas with moisture meters and checks behind baseboards and under cabinets. Air movers and dehumidifiers then dry the structure. The EPA says water-damaged areas and items should be dried within 24 to 48 hours to help prevent mold growth, and it points to keeping indoor humidity below 60 percent.

Higher-value finishes such as hardwood, stone and custom cabinets can sometimes be dried and sometimes need replacement, and a technician can only tell after readings. Our structural drying page explains how drying is checked. The published reference for the trade is the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard.

Insurance and documentation

Ask your insurer what your policy says about the cause of the loss, because the cause usually decides what is covered. The California Department of Insurance Residential Claims Guide says you must report damage to your insurer to start a claim and suggests keeping a log of calls and letters. Photos, moisture readings and an itemized scope help describe the work. They do not decide coverage.
