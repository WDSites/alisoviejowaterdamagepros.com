While you wait

What to do in the first 30 minutes of an emergency

Most people call us with water still running or spreading. These steps are safe for most homes and take only a few minutes. If water touches outlets, a breaker panel or an appliance, stay back and call your utility or 911 before you call us.

Do this first

  • Shut off the main water valve, or the valve for the fixture that is leaking.
  • Turn off power to wet rooms only if you can reach the breaker panel without stepping in water.
  • Lift small furniture, rugs and electronics out of the water. Leave heavy items where they are.
  • Take photos and short videos of every wet surface before anything is moved.
  • Keep children, pets and everyone else out of any water that came from a toilet, drain or sewer.

Know what you are dealing with

Three categories of water, three levels of caution

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard groups water by how contaminated it is, and the category decides how much material comes out. A crew that treats all water the same either wastes money or leaves a health hazard behind.

Water categories and what usually happens
CategoryTypical sourceWhat it means for the job
Category 1Broken supply line, faucet failure, overflowing sink with clean waterMost materials can be dried in place if the response is early. Wet carpet pad is often removed.
Category 2Washing machine or dishwasher discharge, toilet overflow with urine onlyContaminated. Porous materials such as pad and drywall at the floor line are usually removed.
Category 3Sewage backup, rising floodwater, long-standing wet materialsGrossly contaminated. Affected porous materials come out and surfaces are cleaned and treated.

Category can worsen with time. Clean water that sits in wet materials for days may be treated as a higher category.

Insurance claims

General guidance on insurance and water damage

The California Department of Insurance says a standard homeowners policy is not usually the place for flood damage, which is a separate coverage, and that mold or mildew damage that could have been avoided is usually not covered. Sudden and accidental water from a burst pipe or failed appliance is often covered, while a slow leak left alone often is not.

The department also says to report damage to your insurance agent or representative to start a claim and to keep a log of calls and correspondence. Policies differ, so check your policy or ask your insurer what applies to you.

We document the loss for you

Dated photos, moisture readings, drying logs and a written scope are the records adjusters ask about. We keep them, and we do not promise any claim outcome. For policy questions call your insurer or the state insurance department consumer line at 1-800-927-4357.

Local knowledge

Water damage in a planned community built around creeks and canyons

Aliso Viejo is a master-planned city of roughly 6,600 acres that incorporated in 2001. It sits in the Aliso Creek watershed, a 36 square mile drainage that reaches the Pacific Ocean, and the Aliso and Wood Canyons Wilderness Park borders the city. For flood questions about a specific address, the city Public Works department is listed as the floodplain contact, and you can also check the FEMA flood map for your address.

Rain here falls mostly in winter. The National Weather Service record for the former El Toro station shows the heaviest months running from January through March, and its 2023 summary describes the January 9 and 10 atmospheric river that brought 3 to 6 inches to Orange County foothills. Check roof edges, window seals and slider tracks before the wet season, because a small leak found early is a drying job rather than a rebuild.

Water here is imported through the Metropolitan Water District, and Moulton Niguel Water District reports an average hardness of 13.3 grains per gallon in its 2023 report. Hard water can leave white mineral crust around a failing fixture, which can help a technician trace an old leak. Common questions

Do you handle water damage emergencies at night and on weekends in Aliso Viejo?

Yes. The dispatch line is open 24/7, including weekends and holidays. Call and the dispatcher will tell you what to shut off while a crew is arranged, and same-day response is offered when available.

How long does water damage take to dry?

The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth, so speed matters. Drying is finished when moisture readings return to normal for that material, not on a fixed day count.

A small clean-water leak in one room often dries faster than a wet wall cavity or a hardwood floor. Your technician records readings each visit and explains when the equipment can come out.

Will my homeowners insurance pay for water damage?

It depends on the cause and your policy. Sudden and accidental water is often covered, gradual leaks often are not, and flood damage usually needs separate flood coverage. Check your policy or ask your insurer, and ask us for the photos and drying records.

Is water damage restoration the same as waterproofing?

No. Restoration dries and repairs what water already damaged. Waterproofing or a foundation fix prevents future entry and is a different trade. If the source is a structural problem, we will tell you so honestly and suggest you ask a waterproofing or foundation specialist.

I rent, or I live in a condo. Who should I call first?

Tell your property manager or HOA right away and call us to stop the damage in your unit. Take photos, and ask them in writing who authorizes work. Early drying protects your belongings and the shared walls and floors around you.

Do you rent drying equipment?

Our crews place and monitor air movers, dehumidifiers and extraction units as part of a restoration job. Ask the dispatcher if you have a question about anything else.
